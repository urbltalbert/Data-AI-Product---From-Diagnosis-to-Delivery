# 04 · Architecture — Team 7 · Review Insights (P8)

> Describes the prototype as built in `/prototype` on 20 November 2026 (v0). Every line matches a file.

## 1. One-line description
A workflow that, when a hotel asks why its rating dropped, drafts a one-page summary (top 3 negative themes, change vs the previous quarter, two linked quotes each) for the account manager, who approves, edits or writes it herself. Nothing is sent by the system.

## 2. The diagram
```
ticket ─▶ [1 detect] ─▶ [2 eligibility + extract] ─▶ [3 themes + draft] ─▶ [4 policy check] ─▶ ⊘ Claire approves ─▶ sent (person only)
              │ not a rating drop / confidence < 0.70     │ < 50 Q3 reviews · mostly non-English · other hotel_id      │ block
              └───────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────▶ hand over to Claire, with the reason ◀────────┘
⏻ switch: Head of Customer Success sets REVIEW_INSIGHTS=off → every ticket goes to the manual queue; Claire can also click "write it myself" on any ticket
```
Steps 1–4 are in `prototype/pipeline.py`. The send step is not built: it stays a person's action.

## 3. Component table
| # | Component | Rung | Pattern | Trust level | Checkpoint ⊘ | Metric observed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Detect the request (`detect.py`, TF-IDF + logistic regression) | 2 classical ML | routing | L3 | sampling: Claire reviews a sample of detections | recall on rating-drop tickets (first run: 0 / 29 pass 0.70; after spec v2, threshold 0.60 from validation: 30 / 33) |
| 2 | Eligibility and extraction (`rules.py`) | 1 rules | chaining (gate) | L4 | abstention: hand over with reason | correct hand-over reason (7 / 17 first run, 16 / 17 after spec v2) |
| 3 | Themes and draft (`themes.py`, `draft.py`) | v0: lexicon + template (rung 1); brief target: 3 pretrained model | chaining | L2 | approval | top-3 overlap ≥ 2 of 3 (diagnostic: 33 / 33, synthetic data) |
| 4 | Policy check (`policy.py`) | 1 rules | chaining (gate) | L2 | blocks the draft | names / other-hotel ids in drafts (0) |
| 5 | Review and send (not built) | person only | — | person only | approval | drafts sent without approval (must be 0) |

## 4. Workflow or agent?
A workflow. The path is fixed and can be drawn in advance: detect → check → extract → draft → check → person. The model never chooses its next step or its tools, and it has no tool that can contact a hotel. That fits the risk: an error reaches a hotel's decisions, and a guest name is a GDPR problem, so the fence must be code, not a prompt. The brief called it an "agent" because the job chains several steps on the hotel's data; at v0 it is a prompt-chaining workflow with a routing step. To move up (let the model pick how many quarters to read, or which reviews to fetch) we would need a measured gain on the eval-set and a bounded tool list, and the send step would still stay person only.

## 5. Fallbacks
- retrieval finds nothing (no review in the current quarter, or no negative theme): `NO_DRAFT`, hand over (`rules.py`, `pipeline.py`)
- confidence below the threshold (0.60 after spec v2, chosen on `data/tickets_val.csv`): hand over with the confidence shown (`rules.eligibility`); at the first run, with 0.70, this fired on every ticket
- the policy checker blocks the draft: shown with its reason, not approvable (`policy.check`)
- the API is down: not applicable at v0 (no API call); the template drafter is the fallback by design when one is added

## 6. The override switch ⏻
- **Who can switch it off:** Head of Customer Success (day), on-call engineer (night) — roles from `governance.md`
- **Scope:** everyone (global flag); per account manager through "write it myself"
- **Built in:** `prototype/switch.py` (three positions: on · suggest-only · off; an unknown value fails safe to off) and `pipeline.serve_ticket`.
- **Tested on:** 20 November 2026, 11:42 CET, on Enzo Méret's laptop, 7 of 7 checks (`prototype/test_switch.py`, output `evals/rollback_test_2026-11-20_1142.txt`). Not tested: a second person, the night path, the real ticketing tool.

## 7. Data
- Demo / training data: `data/tickets_train.csv` (69 invented tickets, for the detector only) and `data/reviews_synthetic.csv` (8,890 invented reviews, planted themes, generated by `data/make_data.py`, seed 7)
- Evaluation data: `evals/eval_set_v0.csv`, 50 cases. C01–C20 labelled by Ugo and Enzo; C21–C50 drafted with an AI assistant, **to be reviewed by both**. Labels written before the run; never used for tuning.
- Personal data: none in the prototype (all invented). Production: guest names are masked before drafting; see `governance.md`.

## 8. Eval result v0
*"0 % of in-scope inquiries got a draft two graders would send (0 / 33) — the criterion was 75 %."* The detector is under-confident (max 0.69). With step 1 bypassed, steps 2–4 reach 33 / 33 on synthetic data, but also draft on 8 hand-over cases, so the detector cannot simply be removed. Source: `evals/report_v0.md`.

**Update after spec v2 (development run, eval-set already seen):** 30 / 33 on the automatic proxy, but one draft on a hand-over ticket (C07, invoice) — an open safety miss. The result of record needs a new frozen set (Assignment 5) and two human graders. Source: `evals/report_v0.md` §6.

**Update after spec v3 (20 Nov, 50 unseen cases):** a rule-based `ACCOUNT` hand-over (step 2) closes the C07 / C100 family: 0 / 22 drafts on hand-over cases, but only 13 / 28 = 46 % of in-scope tickets get a draft — safe, not useful enough. Source: `evals/report_v0.md` §8.

## 9. What we would build next
1. Raise the detector's recall without losing the floor (46 % of in-scope served at v3): more varied tickets or an LLM classifier behind the same rules, then a new frozen set.
2. Replace the lexicon by an LLM theme extractor and test it against the same cases; then have two humans grade the drafts.
3. Add the Streamlit inbox (approve / edit / write-myself buttons and a timer), which is the MUST feature C of the brief.
